Newfoundland and Labrador Tax Brackets 2026

Newfoundland and Labrador's April budget raised the 2026 basic personal amount to $13,094, and a regulation filed a fortnight later cut the province's low income tax reduction to $842. The first lowers provincial tax for nearly everyone who pays it; the second trims the extra relief at the lowest incomes. Here are the eight 2026 provincial brackets, Ottawa's five, and what the two together charge on each band of income.

More brackets than any other province

A single taxable income carries both schedules, each rate on its own slice. Newfoundland and Labrador divides that income eight ways, and its top three bands start at $285,319, $570,638 and $1,141,275, the last of them far above any threshold another province uses.

The thresholds moved up in January by the province's own consumer price figure. On a dollar past $1,141,275, the federal 33% and the provincial 21.8% add up to 54.80%, the highest combined rate in Canada.

A larger personal amount, a smaller reduction

CRA's January payroll tables went out with $11,188, because the budget didn't come until April 29. To make up the difference, employers withhold on a $15,000 amount from July to December; the return uses $13,094 for the whole year. At the province's 8.7%, that personal amount cancels $1,139 of provincial tax.

The low income tax reduction sits on top of it. For 2026 it is $842, down from $997 in 2025 (Newfoundland and Labrador Regulation 18/26), and it is withdrawn at 16% of net income above $24,191, so nothing is left by $29,454. Between the two, a salary earner owes no provincial tax up to $24,404.

One caveat on that threshold. The Income Tax Act, 2000 indexes it each January, and CRA's 2026 provincial form isn't published yet, so $24,191 is 2025's $23,928 carried forward by the province's 1.1% factor. The Act indexes from the unrounded figure, so the official number could differ by a dollar. A spouse or eligible dependant adds $563 to the reduction and a higher family threshold; the calculators here leave that part out.

Where the reduction is clawed back

A sixteen-cent clawback is steep. On income between $24,191 and $29,454, each extra dollar costs 24.70% in provincial tax rather than 8.7%, and the combined rate of 38.70% is higher than the 36.30% paid at $100,000. Two salaries, one either side of the clawback

On $27,000 of pay the engine charges $1,009 of federal tax and $620 of provincial tax, with the reduction partly spent. On $90,000 the reduction is long gone: $11,252 federal and $8,752 provincial, 22.2% of the salary in income tax.

Common questions

What are the Newfoundland and Labrador tax brackets for 2026?

8.7% up to $44,678, 14.5% up to $89,354, 15.8% up to $159,528, 17.8% up to $223,340, 19.8% up to $285,319, 20.8% up to $570,638, 21.3% up to $1,141,275, and 21.8% beyond.

How does the Newfoundland and Labrador low income tax reduction work?

For 2026 it is $842 for an individual, reduced by 16% of net income over $24,191, and it can lower provincial tax to zero but not below. It is gone by $29,454. A spouse or eligible dependant adds $563 and raises the income threshold.

What basic personal amount does Newfoundland and Labrador use for 2026?

$13,094 on the 2026 return. Payroll used $11,188 until June and a prorated $15,000 from July so that withholding over the year matches $13,094.
