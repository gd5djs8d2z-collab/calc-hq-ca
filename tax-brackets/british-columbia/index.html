British Columbia Tax Brackets 2026

British Columbia's lowest income tax rate for 2026 is 5.60%, but payroll software that loaded CRA's January tables started the year withholding 5.06%, last year's rate. The province's February budget raised it after those tables had gone out. Here are this year's federal and B.C. brackets as they now stand, and what each band of income really pays once the B.C. tax reduction is counted.

Ottawa's five brackets and B.C.'s seven

Both schedules apply to the same taxable income at the same time, and each rate reaches only the slice of income inside its own band. B.C. cuts its schedule finer than any province except Newfoundland and Labrador, into seven bands, with the top rate of 20.5% starting at $265,545.

Every B.C. threshold above went up this January with the province's consumer price index. That is the last increase for a while. Budget 2026 paused indexation for the 2027 to 2030 tax years, so these seven thresholds stay put until 2031 while the federal ones keep rising. A pay increase that only matches inflation will move a little more income into B.C.'s higher bands in each of those four years.

Why 2026 began at the wrong rate

The budget tabled on February 17 raised B.C.'s lowest rate from 5.06% to 5.60% for 2026 and every year after. CRA's payroll tables for January 1 were already in use with the old rate, so through June most employers withheld on the bottom band at 5.06%. The edition effective July 1 makes up the difference by withholding 6.14% on that band for the rest of the year. Halfway between the two is 5.60%, which is the point: 6.14% is a catch-up device for the second half, not a rate anyone owes, and the return taxes the whole year at 5.60%.

B.C. values the basic personal amount and most other non-refundable credits at its lowest rate, so the same change raised what they are worth. The $13,216 basic personal amount now removes $740 of B.C. tax. For anyone whose income stays inside the first band, the higher rate and the larger credit partly cancel.

The reduction that taxes low incomes at 9.16%

B.C. cancels up to $690 of provincial tax for lower incomes through the B.C. tax reduction. The whole amount goes to anyone with net income up to $25,570. Past that, it shrinks by 3.56% of each extra dollar and is gone at $44,952. Each of those lost cents is B.C. tax, so on that stretch the province takes 9.16% of a dollar rather than 5.60%, and the combined rate of 23.16% is higher than the 19.60% that applies just past $44,952.

The reduction also hides B.C.'s basic personal amount from view. On its own, the credit would start B.C. tax once income passed $13,216. With the reduction on top, there is no B.C. tax at all until income passes $25,537. The calculators here test the reduction against taxable income, which is the same figure as net income for a salary or a pension with no special deductions.

Paid as salary, the same dollar is cheaper up to $85,000, because a paycheque dollar brings contribution credits with it and the enhanced CPP is deducted before tax is worked out. What $85,000 of pay owes

On an $85,000 salary the engine charges $10,227 of federal tax and $4,401 of B.C. tax, $14,627 together, which is 17.2% of the salary. The marginal rate on that pay is 28.20%: the federal 20.5% plus B.C.'s 7.70%. Only the dollars above $58,523 pay that federal rate, though, and only those above $50,363 pay that B.C. one. Everything underneath sits in a cheaper band or under a credit.

CPP and EI are both at their annual maximums by $85,000, so a $5,000 raise from there loses nothing to them. It adds $1,410 of income tax and keeps the rest, because it stays inside the same two bands.

Two basic personal amounts

Ottawa forgives tax on the first $16,452 of income through its basic personal amount; B.C. forgives it on the first $13,216. Each government turns its amount into a credit at its own lowest rate:

Common questions

What is the lowest income tax rate in British Columbia for 2026?

5.60% on the first $50,363 of taxable income. B.C.'s February budget raised it for 2026, after CRA's January payroll tables had gone out, so withholding on that band runs higher from July to make up the first half of the year. Your return applies 5.60% to the whole year.

Why is the B.C. marginal rate higher at $35,000 than at $47,000?

Because of the B.C. tax reduction. It shrinks by 3.56% of every dollar of net income between $25,570 and $44,952, which lifts the combined federal and B.C. rate on that stretch to 23.16%. Once the reduction is used up the rate falls back to 19.60% until B.C.'s second bracket begins at $50,363.

Are B.C. tax brackets indexed to inflation?

They were for 2026, by the B.C. consumer price index. Budget 2026 pauses indexation for the 2027 to 2030 tax years, so the thresholds stay at their 2026 levels until it resumes in 2031. The federal brackets keep indexing every January, so the two schedules will drift further apart each year until then.
