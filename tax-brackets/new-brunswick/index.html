New Brunswick Tax Brackets 2026

New Brunswick's low-income tax reduction is withdrawn at only 3% a dollar, so it reaches further up the income scale than any other province's: worth $818 to a single filer, it doesn't run out until $49,625. That stretch, more than half of the province's first bracket, is taxed at a combined 26.40% instead of 23.40%. Below are the federal and New Brunswick brackets for 2026 and the rate on every band.

New Brunswick indexed its thresholds, its personal amounts and the reduction itself this January, by the same factor. The second bracket starts at $52,333, a few thousand dollars below the federal $58,523; the top one, at 19.5%, from $193,861.

How the reduction is built

The Income Tax Act defines the reduction rather than naming it. Take the gap between the reduction's income threshold, $22,358 for 2026, and the $13,664 basic personal amount; value it at the 9.4% lowest rate and round up. That gives $818, which is the provincial tax a single person with no other credits would owe at the threshold, rounded up to the dollar. So below $22,358 of net income, New Brunswick takes nothing; above it, the reduction shrinks by 3% of each extra dollar. With CPP and EI credits added, a salary pays no provincial tax up to $23,611.

The 2026 figures are worked out from the Act, not read off a published table: CRA's 2026 form arrives in January, and the province's own page stops at 2025's $21,920. Carried forward by New Brunswick's 2.0% indexing factor, the threshold becomes $22,358, and the same calculation that gave 2025's $802 gives $818. A spouse or an eligible dependant doubles the reduction; the calculators here count only the single-filer part.

$40,000 in Moncton

At $40,000 of salary the reduction is still worth $300. The engine charges $2,691 of federal tax and $1,910 of New Brunswick tax, 11.5% of the salary. A $5,000 raise from there adds $1,230 of income tax, part of it the reduction being withdrawn.

Common questions

What are the New Brunswick tax brackets for 2026?

9.4% on taxable income up to $52,333, 14% up to $104,666, 16% up to $193,861, and 19.5% above that, plus federal tax.

Who gets the New Brunswick low-income tax reduction?

A single New Brunswick resident with net income under $49,625 gets some of it. A single person gets $818 at incomes up to $22,358, less 3% of income above that. It can bring New Brunswick tax down to zero but is not paid out beyond it.

Why is my New Brunswick marginal rate lower at $51,000 than at $35,000?

Because at $35,000 the low-income tax reduction is still being withdrawn, adding 3% to the 9.4% provincial rate. It runs out at $49,625, and the combined rate falls to 23.40% until the next bracket.
