Alberta Tax Brackets 2026

Until 2025, Alberta taxed the first dollar of income at 10%. It now charges 8% on the first $61,200 of taxable income and saves 10% for what lies above, and its $22,769 basic personal amount is still the largest any province or territory offers. Here are the 2026 federal and Alberta brackets, and then what both together charge on each band, which in Alberta comes close to the plain sum of the two schedules.

Six Alberta bands under five federal ones

Alberta and Ottawa each tax your whole taxable income, but every rate touches only the dollars that land in its band. Alberta's lower thresholds sit close to Ottawa's without matching them, which is what produces the short bands in the combined table further down.

Both schedules were indexed this January. Alberta's 8% band ends at $61,200, a little past the federal $58,523 where Ottawa's rate steps from 14% to 20.5%. For the $2,677 in between, the higher federal rate meets the lower Alberta one, and the next dollar costs 28.50% before rising to 30.50%.

What the 8% bracket did and didn't change

Charging 8% rather than 10% on the first $61,200 is worth up to $1,224 a year, which anyone with taxable income of $61,200 or more receives in full. Alberta's non-refundable credits are valued at its lowest rate, though, so the same cut made every credit worth less. The $22,769 personal amount now removes $1,822 of Alberta tax, where at 10% it removed $2,277. The saving that is left is still real, and it is largest for incomes past $61,200.

To protect people with very large credit claims, Alberta added a supplemental credit that applies only when total non-refundable credits pass $61,200. A claim made up of the basic personal amount with CPP and EI comes nowhere near that, so the calculators here leave it out.

The rate on each next dollar

Alberta has no surtax, premium or income-tested reduction, so the table below differs from simple addition in only two places: the stretches where one government's personal amount has run out and the other's hasn't, and the federal taper near the top. Even so, it was produced the long way, by stepping calc-hq's tax engine through every income and reading off the cost of the next dollar. It covers federal and Alberta income tax on income with no payroll contributions attached, such as interest, rent or pension income.

The 14.00% row near the top is the one worth a second look. Ottawa starts taxing at $16,452 and Alberta at $22,769, so between those two figures only the federal 14% applies, and Alberta's 8% joins only once its larger personal amount is used up.

On wages the bottom of the table runs a little cheaper, because CPP and EI are paid on a salary dollar and earn a credit, and its enhanced CPP is taken off taxable income. At a $60,000 salary the next dollar of pay is taxed at 26.77% rather than the table's 28.50%. From $85,000 up, salary and table rates are the same, with every threshold reached $1,127 further up the pay scale.

A $95,000 salary, slice by slice

On $95,000 of pay, the engine charges $12,277 of federal tax and $5,970 of Alberta tax. Together that is $18,247, or 19.2% of the salary, although the top dollar is taxed at 30.50%. The gap between those two figures is the whole idea of a bracket: only the income above $61,200 pays Alberta's 10%, only the income above $58,523 pays the federal 20.5%, and the personal amounts cancel the tax on the bottom slices outright.

CPP and EI have both stopped by $95,000, so another $1,000 of salary adds $305 of income tax and nothing else.

Alberta's personal amount is larger than Ottawa's

Most provinces set their basic personal amount below Ottawa's. Saskatchewan's is $3,929 above it and Quebec's $2,500; Alberta's is $6,317 above it, more than any province or territory:

Common questions

What are the Alberta tax brackets for 2026?

8% on taxable income up to $61,200, 10% up to $154,259, 12% up to $185,111, 13% up to $246,813, 14% up to $370,220 and 15% beyond. On top of that comes federal tax, from 14% to 33%.

Does Alberta charge a surtax or a health premium?

No. Alberta has neither, so its combined marginal rate is the federal rate plus the Alberta rate for that band, apart from the stretch between $16,452 and $22,769 where only federal tax applies and the federal personal-amount taper above $181,440. The top combined rate is 48%, on income over $370,220.

When did the 8% Alberta tax bracket start?

With the 2025 tax year. It applies to the first $61,200 of taxable income for 2026, the threshold having been indexed in January. Alberta's non-refundable credits are valued at 8% as well, which takes back part of the saving.
