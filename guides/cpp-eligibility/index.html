Who Qualifies for the Canada Pension Plan?

The CPP retirement pension has a low bar: be 60 or older and have made at least one valid contribution. The harder part is that nothing arrives on its own. Unlike Old Age Security, CPP has to be applied for, and the disability and survivor benefits have stricter tests and back-payment limits that make timing matter.

The retirement pension

Two conditions: you're at least 60, and you've made at least one valid contribution. A contribution can come from your own work in Canada or from credits split to you by a spouse or common-law partner after a divorce or separation. A short working life still qualifies; it just produces a small pension, which the CPP calculator shows how contributions build.

Starting the pension doesn't mean you have to stop working, and it isn't reduced if you keep going. If you're under 70, still working and still contributing, each year of contributions adds a post-retirement benefit, paid automatically from the following year for life. You can opt out of those contributions from 65, and they end at 70 whether you're working or not.

If you lived and worked only in Quebec, or worked there and live there now, the Quebec Pension Plan applies instead, through Retraite Québec. Years in a country covered by one of Canada's international social security agreements can lead to a pension from both.

You have to apply

The retirement pension isn't paid until you apply for it; the one automatic switch is a CPP disability benefit, which becomes a retirement pension at 65. You can apply up to 12 months before the month you want the pension to begin, online through My Service Canada Account, where a decision comes by mail within 28 days, or on paper with form ISP-1000, which takes up to 120 days. Some people must use paper, including anyone living outside Canada or whose account is managed by a trustee or power of attorney. Choosing the start age is its own decision; CPP at 60 vs 65 compares the options.

Leaving it too long has a hard edge. If you die in or before the month of your 70th birthday without having applied, the retirement pension can't be paid to anyone. After 70, your estate can apply within a year of the death, but it receives only the month of death and the 11 months before it, and nothing for months up to your 70th birthday.

Disability benefits

CPP disability has three tests, and you must meet all of them:

  • Age: between 18 and 65.
  • Disability: a physical or mental condition that stops you working at any job on a regular basis, is long-term, and isn't expected to improve or is likely to cause death.
  • Contributions: Service Canada looks at the six years before the disability stopped you working. You need contributions in four of them, or in three if you have 25 years of contributions in total.

Falling short on contributions isn't always the end. Credits earned in another country, the child-rearing provision for years you worked less to care for young children, and a credit split after a divorce or separation can all help, and someone who didn't apply when they first became disabled can still qualify if they had enough contributions back then. Dependent children of a disability recipient can get the children's benefit if they're under 18, or 18 to 25 and in school. At 65 the disability benefit turns into a retirement pension automatically.

The survivor's pension

It goes to the legal spouse or common-law partner of a contributor who has died, with common-law meaning at least a year of living together. A separated legal spouse can qualify if the contributor had no common-law partner, unless a credit split between the two was approved from January 2025 onward. Remarrying doesn't end it, and someone widowed more than once gets the larger pension only.

At 65 or older, a survivor with no other CPP benefit receives 60% of the contributor's retirement pension; below 65, it's a flat-rate amount plus 37.5%. Apply soon after the death: back payments are limited to 12 months, the month you apply plus the 11 before it.

Common questions

Can I get CPP if I only worked a few years?

Yes. The retirement pension needs only one valid contribution and an age of at least 60. Fewer years of contributions mean a smaller pension, not no pension.

Do I have to apply for CPP?

Yes. The CPP retirement pension is not paid automatically. You can apply up to 12 months before you want it to start, online or on paper, and an online application gets a decision within 28 days.

Who qualifies for CPP disability benefits?

People aged 18 to 65 with a long-term disability that stops them working at any job on a regular basis, who contributed to CPP in four of the last six years, or three of the last six with at least 25 years of contributions in total.
