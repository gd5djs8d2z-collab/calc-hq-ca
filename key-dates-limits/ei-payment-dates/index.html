EI Payment Dates: How the Timing Works

Unlike CPP or the Canada child benefit, Employment Insurance has no list of payment dates. Each claim runs on its own two-week cycle: you file a report, Service Canada processes it, and the payment follows a few business days later. So the date your money arrives depends on when your claim started and when you send each report, not on a government calendar.

From applying to the first payment

Service Canada's standard is a decision within 28 days of the application, a target it aims to meet 80% of the time. If you're eligible and nothing is missing, the first payment comes about 28 days after you apply. The usual causes of delay are missing records of employment, which employers are supposed to issue within five calendar days of your final pay period, and documents Service Canada has asked for.

Shortly after you apply, a benefit statement arrives by mail with a four-digit access code. You need it, with your social insurance number, to file the reports that release each payment, so the first payment can't be issued until it has arrived and the first report is in.

The waiting week

The first week of an EI claim isn't paid. Service Canada calls it the waiting period and compares it to an insurance deductible. Benefits are paid for the weeks that come after it.

Every two weeks, after your report

While you're on EI you complete a report every two weeks, online or by phone, covering any work, earnings, training and your availability for each week. Payments are only issued once a report is processed. When you submit one, you're told the date the next is due; you can't file before that date, and you have three weeks after it to do so. Because nothing is paid until the report is processed, a report filed late delays the money it covers.

With direct deposit, the money reaches your account two to three business days after you complete the report. That makes your own reporting day the thing to plan around: if your report is due on a Monday and you file it that morning, expect the deposit around midweek.

Claims that don't need reports

Maternity, parental and caregiving claims, apprentices and people on a Work-Sharing agreement can ask to be exempted from reporting. Their payments then arrive without a report each fortnight, but they must still tell Service Canada about any work, money or change that affects the claim. If you're on maternity or parental benefits, the EI maternity and parental calculator shows the weekly amount and how the weeks stack up.

Common questions

Does EI have payment dates?

No. There is no published EI payment calendar. Payments follow each claimant's two-week reporting cycle, and with direct deposit they arrive two to three business days after the report is completed.

How long does it take to get the first EI payment?

About 28 days after applying, if you are eligible and have provided everything Service Canada needs. The first week of the claim is an unpaid waiting period.

Why is my EI payment late?

Usually because the report covering those weeks has not been filed or processed yet, or because Service Canada needs more information. Payments are only issued after a report is processed.
